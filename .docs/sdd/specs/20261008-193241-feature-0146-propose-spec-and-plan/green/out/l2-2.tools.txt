Here's the path of the March calendar file, from your request to the file on disk.

**What the example starts with:** the coworking has five reservations saved. Four are in March and one is in April. One of the March ones, Norte on 12 March, is cancelled.

1. **You ask for March.** The tool takes the month you typed. It doesn't check that the month is valid or that you gave one.
2. **The tool reads the saved reservations.** It loads the whole list of reservations, for every month, from the coworking's data file.
3. **It keeps only what belongs in the file.** The April reservation goes, and so does the cancelled March one. The three left are Norte on 2 March (10:00–12:00), Sur on 5 March (16:00–17:00) and Norte on 19 March (12:00–13:30). They're sorted by day and then by start time.
4. **Each reservation becomes a calendar entry.** The entry is titled "Sala Norte" or "Sala Sur". Its start and end come from the time slot, shifted back two hours so they're written in universal time. Norte on 2 March goes from 10:00–12:00 to 08:00–10:00.
5. **The entries are wrapped into one calendar file.** The standard calendar header and footer go around the three entries. Google Calendar and Outlook both understand this format.
6. **The file is saved and its location is shown.** The tool creates the exports folder if it's missing and saves the file under the month's name. It prints that location on screen.

After that, you open the file or import it into your calendar. That step happens outside the tool.

Two things in step 4 look wrong to me.
- The two-hour shift is fixed. The coworking's clock moves to summer time on 29 March, so for most of March the real difference is one hour, not two.
- The converted times are written without the marker that says "universal time". Calendars may read them as local time, so Norte's 10:00 could show up at 08:00 in your calendar.

I haven't run it against a real calendar, so that second point is from reading the code. The product description defines the time slot as being in the coworking's time, and the code doesn't do that. The tool also has no way to export a single room, even though a spec for that exists.

**Where to look**
- Step 1, the command and month: `src/cli.js`
- The whole path in order: `src/export/index.js`
- Step 2, reading the reservations: `src/bookings/repository.js`, data in `data/bookings.json`
- Step 3, filtering and sorting: `src/export/filter.js`
- Steps 4 and 5, entries and the file: `src/export/ics.js`
- Step 6, saving: `src/export/writer.js`. The file ends up at `exports/2026-03.ics`.
